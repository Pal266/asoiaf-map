# Ice — summary

- **What it is:** the world's ice sheets and glaciers, with their thickness. They are found together with the climate,
  because ice cools the climate that keeps it.
- **The model:**
  - a degree-day surface mass balance (snowfall against melt) on the 10 km grid;
  - ice sheets grown until their accumulation balances melt and calving;
  - perfectly plastic ice thickness over the bed;
  - the temperature and moisture models rerun on each new surface.
- **Earth test:**
  - from today's surface, the balance is positive over about two thirds of Earth's ice, with few false alarms;
  - from bare ground, Antarctica regrows and Greenland does not, as in published models;
  - known faults: too much ice on high plateaux (Tibet) and too little on small maritime ice caps.
- **The world:**
  - the south polar continent is under an Antarctic-sized ice sheet (14 M km²), as its geological history implies;
  - there are smaller ice sheets on a high plateau of the south-western continent, at 40° S, and on a polar island;
  - **the north has no ice sheet** (canon deviation 6). The model's northern ice is all or nothing, and the owner
    chose "nothing". The Land of Always Winter is polar tundra on permafrost, with a mountain ice cap; the haunted
    forest and the North are free of ice.
