# Geological history — summary

A dated scenario of about 3 Gyr (data/history.json) with two supercontinent cycles explains the known world
by Earth analogues: Caledonian north (420 Ma), Variscan Mountains of the Moon and Uralian Bone Mountains (300 Ma),
breakup and the Sunset Sea (from 180 Ma), Dorne's Pyrenean collision (60–40 Ma), the narrow sea reopening
on the old suture like the Red Sea (25 Ma), and the young arcs of Valyria and the Jade Sea.

The layer stores its present-day imprint on the 10 km grid: crust type, eight continental province types, ocean floor and oceanic plateaus, age of the
last tectonic event, ocean floor age and basement depth. Continental crust covers 40.1% of the planet
(Earth ~41%), the mean ocean floor age is 60 Myr (Earth 64) with six active and two extinct ridges, oceanic plateaus cover 5.1% of the ocean (as on Earth). Every canon feature has its E05 province.

The history is scripted, not simulated; continental ages are set by block. Details: experiments/E07_geological_history.md.
