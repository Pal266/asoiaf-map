# Geology (rock ages, faults, seismic hazard) — summary

- **What it is:** the age of the surface rock of every land cell (by geological period), the faults with their
  type and activity, and a seismic hazard map in the four classes of the GSHAP map.
- **Ages:**
  - crystalline rocks take the age of the tectonic event that made their province;
  - sediments follow the global area-age law of exposed rock (Wilkinson et al. 2009) within the limits of their
    setting;
  - arc volcanics are young, and the Doom's ash is 400 years old;
  - the world's eras come out close to Earth's: Cenozoic 56 %, Precambrian 23 %.
- **Faults:**
  - plate boundaries: subduction, ridges, transforms;
  - contacts of tectonic provinces: thrusts, normal faults, sutures;
  - each is active, dormant or inactive by the age of its last movement.
- **Hazard:** plate boundaries weighted by type and speed, plus active deforming zones. Calibrated on Earth's plate
  boundaries (PB2002) against GSHAP: 87 % of 71 cities right.
- **The world:**
  - Westeros is quiet: worn Caledonian and Variscan faults, dormant Red Mountains and Narrow Sea margins;
  - southern Dorne (the Dorne transform), Volantis and Valyria (the Summer Sea trench) and Asshai are very high;
  - southern Essos's collision belt is high.
