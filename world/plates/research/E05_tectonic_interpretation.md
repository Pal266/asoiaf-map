# E05. Tectonic interpretation of canon geography (present-day plates)

## Goal

For every canon feature of `world/canon_relief/data/canon_features.json` and the canon outline, find a tectonic
setting that produces it on Earth and a real analogue, and assemble them into a consistent set of present-day plates.
This is the target state of the geological history (stage 2); the history itself (stage 3) must end in it.

Method: design "backwards from canon" (the plates are chosen to explain the map), checked against Earth analogues
(Rule 1). Nothing here is drawn yet; geometry follows once the interpretation is agreed.

## The picture in one paragraph

The known world reads like a mirror of western Eurasia and its neighbours. **Westeros and Essos** are pulled apart by a
young ocean, **the narrow sea** (like the Red Sea). **Sothoryos** drives north into Essos across a closing sea, **the
Summer Sea** (like Africa and the Mediterranean): a belt of collision mountains runs along the south of the two
continents (Red Mountains — Painted Mountains — Ghiscar), with a volcanic arc above the sinking Summer Sea floor at
**Valyria** (like the Aegean arc and Santorini). Far to the east, **Ulthos** drives north into the Shadow Lands across
the Jade Sea's island arcs (like Australia and Indonesia). Inside Essos, **the Bone Mountains** mark an old seam between
two ancient blocks (like the Urals), lifted again by the far-field push of these collisions (like the Tian Shan). The
west coast of Westeros faces the Pacific-sized **Sunset Sea** quietly: it is a passive margin, with no volcanoes.

## Present-day plates

| Plate | Contains | Boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| **Westeros** | Westeros (with Dorne welded on), the west half of the narrow sea floor, the east half of the Sunset Sea floor | spreading ridge in the narrow sea (east); passive west coast; a spreading ridge in mid Sunset Sea (west) |
| **Essos** | Essos west and east of the Bone Mountains (one plate, old internal suture), Ibben | narrow-sea ridge (west); subduction of the Summer Sea under Valyria and collision along the south (south); arc–continent collision with Ulthos and active margin facing the Sunset Sea (east) |
| **Sothoryos** | Sothoryos, the Summer Sea floor, the Summer Isles | converges with Essos (north) |
| **Ulthos** | Ulthos | converges with the Shadow Lands across the Jade Sea arcs (north) |
| **Sunset** (one or more oceanic plates) | the west half of the Sunset Sea floor | mid-Sunset ridge (east); subduction under the far eastern shores of Essos and Ulthos (west), closing the ring around the known world |
| **Southern continent**, **South polar** | the unknown continent in the south of the Sunset Sea; the south polar continent (decision 6) | to be set by the geological history |

## Open questions for the project owner

1. **Is the narrow sea still opening?** Recommended: yes, slowly (a few mm/yr, like the Red Sea), so Westeros and Essos
   are separate plates and the young rift explains the narrow sea, the Stepstones and Dragonmont. Consequence: mild
   earthquakes along the narrow sea, which canon neither mentions nor rules out.
2. **Dorne as a former separate block** (like Iberia), now welded to Westeros along the Red Mountains. Recommended:
   yes; it explains why Dorne differs and why its border is a mountain wall.
3. **The analogue frame** (narrow sea ≈ Red Sea; Summer Sea ≈ Mediterranean; Sothoryos ≈ Africa; Ulthos ≈ Australia;
   Bone Mountains ≈ Urals + Tian Shan). Recommended as the backbone of the geological history; it reuses well-studied
   Earth processes for every feature.

## Decision

The project owner agreed to all three (2026-11-18): the narrow sea is still opening slowly, so Westeros and Essos are
separate plates; Dorne is a former separate block welded to Westeros along the Red Mountains; the analogue frame above
is the backbone of the geological history.
