# Tectonics — summary (layer 3)

A supercontinent made of 11 blocks (cratons and accreted terranes, welded 410–220 Ma) covers 41 % of the
planet. At 100 Ma it breaks along its shorter assembly sutures into 9 continental plates and 3 microcontinents; the exterior ocean is
divided among 7 oceanic plates. Rigid plates then drift for 100 Myr in 1 Myr steps:
- continents disperse from one another, and oceanic plates move towards them and subduct;
- plate motions are reorganised at 70 and 40 Ma;
- the simulation produces new ocean floor, continental and island arcs, rifted margins and collision orogens;
  buoyant arcs and plateaus accrete to the continents they meet;
- plates weld after ~0.5 M km² of convergence and then take a new motion; the collision zone keeps converging for
  40 Myr inside the welded plate, thickening and widening;
- continental area is conserved up to collisional loss and shortening (3 %, partly offset by accretion), and
  crustal thickness is carried and thickened in collisions;
- slowly drifting mantle plumes add large igneous provinces (3 at the breakup, 7 in all), oceanic plateaus, hotspot tracks and,
  for the 40 hotspots active today, swells; volcano centres ride along the tracks (E05).

The present day:
- **Landmasses:** five large continents above sea level, 37, 24, 22, 19 and 11 M km², and several of 1–3 M km².
- **Ocean floor:** mean age 50 Myr.
- **Sea level:** +61 m. Land covers 24 % of the surface, and 39 % of the continental crust lies under shelf seas.
- **Mountains:** young collision orogens 8 % of the continental crust, plateaus 1 %, continental arcs 15 %.

Speeds, crust area, ocean age, sea level and thickness are calibrated against Earth (see E01–E04 and
`research/`). The layer writes `tectonics.npz` in the format of the first world's history layer, plus crustal
thickness, plates, a first isostatic surface, the present plate boundaries and the volcanoes (hotspot centres and
arc fronts).

The spin axis is moved 8.0° towards less polar land (decision 7, at most 15°): 11 % of the land is poleward of 60°.

Open points: see E03–E05; volcanic islands are left to the relief layer.
