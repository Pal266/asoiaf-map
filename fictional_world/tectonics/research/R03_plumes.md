# R03 — Mantle plumes, hotspots and large igneous provinces

- **Hotspots.** Courtillot et al. (2003, EPSL 205, 295) list 49 hotspots. Only 7 meet criteria for a very deep
  origin ("primary"): 3 in the Pacific, 4 in the Indo-Atlantic hemisphere. The others come from shallower
  boundary layers. Sleep (1990) listed 37.
- **Large igneous provinces (LIPs).** The long-term average is about one event every 20 Myr, clustered at times of
  supercontinent breakup (Bryan & Ferrari 2013, GSA Bull. 125, 1053).
  - LIPs typically form in one pulse of ~1–5 Myr, or several such pulses.
  - Crust under oceanic plateaus is 10–35 km thick; LIP crust generally 20–40 km (Coffin & Eldholm 1994,
    Rev. Geophys. 32, 1).
  - The Ontong Java Plateau covers 1.86 M km².
- **Hotspot swells.** The Hawaiian swell is ~1 km high and ~1000 km wide; the swell is visible in a band of
  half-width ~500 km for ~2000 km downstream of the hotspot (Van Ark & Lin 2004, JGR 109, B11401). A stationary
  plate gets a dome-shaped swell; a moving plate drags the swell along downstream. Individual volcanic edifices
  are ~100 km across (King & Adam 2014).

Used in E05 (in `drift.py` and `output.py`):
- 7 primary plumes with LIP heads, 3 of them within 10 Myr of the breakup;
- secondary plumes active for 10–60 Myr;
- tracks thicken the crust;
- swells 1 km / 0.5 km high, half-width 500 km.
