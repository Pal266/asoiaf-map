# Relief — summary (layer 4)

Bedrock elevation (the mean of each 10 km cell) and the relief inside each cell, for the whole planet.
- **Continents** stand at the height their crustal thickness gives (Airy isostasy). The layer adds a plain texture
  by province and ridges and valleys that grow with elevation and run along the strike of the belts.
- **Flooded continental margins** get a shelf to 130 m and a slope to the ocean floor.
- **The ocean floor** follows its age (GDH1), with sediment fitted to GlobSed, trenches, residual depth and abyssal
  hills.
- **Volcanoes** are built on the hotspot centres, with summits subsiding with age as along the Hawaiian chain, and on
  the arc fronts.

The result:
- land 23.6 % of the surface, mean height 625 m, highest cell 5829 m;
- mean ocean depth 3408 m;
- shelf 29.1 M km² (Earth 28.4 M);
- 90 hotspot islands and 331 arc islands.

The ocean is shallower than Earth's because the floor is young and the sea stands 60 m higher, a Cretaceous-like
state. The land is lower than Earth's, which has ice sheets.
