# R01 — Earth references for the relief

**Hypsometry (Earth, 5′ grid; stevedutch.net "Earth hypsometry").**
- Land at 0–1000 m: 110.3 M km² (21.6 % of the surface); 1000–2000 m: 23.5 M km² (4.6 %); above 2000 m: 3.5 % of the
  surface.
- So ~27 % of the land lies above 1000 m and ~12 % above 2000 m. The Antarctic and Greenland ice surfaces are
  included; bedrock alone is lower.
- Ocean by depth bands (% of ocean): 0–500 m 10.2, 500–2500 m 9.2, 2500–4000 m 27.5, 4000–5000 m 33.2,
  5000–6000 m 19.2, >6000 m ~0.6 (the first world's E08 used the same table).
- Mean land elevation 840 m and mean ocean depth 3688 m (Britannica); the land is ~29 % of the surface (Eakins &
  Sharman 2012: 0.2905).
- Shelf (sea floor above 200 m): 28.4 M km² (land plus shelf 177.4 M km² minus land 149 M km²).

**Sea-floor sediment** (GlobSed, Straume et al. 2019, as fitted in the first world's E08): continental margins,
12.9 % of the ocean, hold 3044 m on average; the far ocean 404 m; 337 M km³ in all. The fit is
min(3.25 m/Myr × age, 1 km) + 3134 m × exp(−(d/554 km)²), with d the distance from land. The floor rises by 0.57 of
the sediment thickness (isostasy).

**Hotspot volcanoes** (Hawaii–Emperor chain). Summits of the main islands fall with age:
- Mauna Kea 4207 m (0–1 Ma), Haleakala 3055 m (~1.3 Ma), Kaala 1220 m (Oahu, ~3 Ma), Kawaikini 1598 m (Kauai, ~5 Ma);
- Nihoa 272 m (~7 Ma), Necker 84 m (~10 Ma), French Frigate Shoals at sea level (~12 Ma);
- the Emperor seamounts (40–80 Ma) lie 1–3 km below the sea, still ~3 km above the floor.

The Big Island is ~130 km across. Secondary hotspots: Teide 3715 m, Fogo 2829 m, Pico 2351 m.

**Arc volcanoes.** Volcanic fronts lie ~100–300 km behind the trench (Syracuse & Abers 2006), with volcanoes ~50–70 km
apart (de Bremond d'Ars et al. 1995). Island-arc summits: Lesser Antilles ~1.2–1.5 km, Aleutians up to ~2.8 km.
